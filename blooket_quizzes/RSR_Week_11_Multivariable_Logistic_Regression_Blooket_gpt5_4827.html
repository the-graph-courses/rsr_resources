Answer Key · 1–8

Short explanations for facilitator review
Section: pre-work review
Answers: 1–8
1CChange in log odds.
A logistic coefficient is additive on the log-odds scale, not the probability scale.
2BOR ≈ 1.12.
Exponentiating 0.1168 gives 1.124, or about 12% higher odds per year.
3D25% lower odds.
For an OR below 1, the percent lower is (1 − OR) × 100%.
4ATwo coefficients.
A three-level factor uses one reference level and two comparisons.
5C0.80.
If P(pass) is 0.20, then P(fail) is 1 − 0.20 = 0.80.
6BMaximize the likelihood.
MLE chooses coefficients that make the outcomes actually observed as likely as possible.
7D240.
Deviance = −2 × (−120) = 240.
8AIntercept-only model.
The null model predicts from the overall outcome rate without predictors.

Answer Key · 9–15

Short explanations for facilitator review
Sections: AIC + workshop bridge
Answers: 9–15
9CModel B fits better.
Lower deviance indicates better fit, but adding parameters can lower deviance even when they add little value.
10BAIC = deviance + 2k.
For this logistic model formula, k counts all estimated coefficients, including the intercept.
11DSame outcome and rows.
The linear-age and quadratic-age candidates use the same data and likelihood family.
12AWeak evidence.
An AIC difference below about 2 usually gives little reason to strongly prefer one candidate.
13Cglm(..., family = binomial).
The outcome goes on the left; all predictors enter on the right with plus signs.
14B4% higher adjusted odds.
“Adjusted” means the comparison holds the other variables in the model constant. Association is not automatically causation.
15DLinearity is on the log-odds scale.
Normal residuals and constant residual variance are linear-regression assumptions, not binary logistic-regression requirements.
