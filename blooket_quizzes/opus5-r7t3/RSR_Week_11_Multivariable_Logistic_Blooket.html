Section A: pre-work review likelihood, deviance and AIC · 7 questions

1A logistic model's log-likelihood is a score for how well the model matches the data. Which direction means a better fit?
  1. a) Higher (less negative) is better
  2. b) Lower (more negative) is better
  3. c) Closest to zero from either side is better
  4. d) It does not indicate fit, only sample size
2logLik() returns −1727.6 for a model. What is that model's deviance?
  1. a) 3455.1
  2. b) −3455.1
  3. c) 1727.6
  4. d) −863.8

Deviance = −2 × log-likelihood. The sign flips, so lower deviance means higher likelihood means better fit.

3In a glm() summary, the "Null deviance" line describes which model?
  1. a) The intercept-only model, y ~ 1
  2. b) The model you actually fitted
  3. c) A model with all possible predictors
  4. d) A model fitted to a null hypothesis dataset
4Why can you not choose between two models just by picking the lower residual deviance?
  1. a) Adding predictors almost always lowers it, even useless ones
  2. b) Residual deviance can be negative
  3. c) It only works for linear models
  4. d) It ignores the outcome variable
5A model has residual deviance 305.2 and estimates 10 parameters. What is its AIC?
  1. a) 325.2
  2. b) 315.2
  3. c) 610.4
  4. d) 30.5

AIC = residual deviance + 2k, so 305.2 + 20 = 325.2. This is the actual multivariable model we fit in the workshop.

6Your model has an AIC of 322. On its own, what does that number tell you?
  1. a) Nothing. Only differences between models are meaningful
  2. b) The model fits well, since it is under 500
  3. c) The model fits badly, since it is over 100
  4. d) 32.2% of variation is explained

And those differences only mean something when the models share the same outcome and the same rows. Watch for this when a predictor has missing values.

7You are unsure how to enter a continuous predictor. What is the better strategy?
  1. a) Compare a few forms supported by theory or prior studies
  2. b) Try every form you can think of and report the lowest AIC
  3. c) Always use the most flexible form available
  4. d) Always split it into equal-width groups

Section C: checking a logistic model 6 questions

13 Which SUNSHINE check carries no meaning for a logistic regression?image
images/sunshine_checks_linear.png · the linear version on purpose, so the answer is not printed on the slide. The logistic version is also in the folder for the reveal.
  1. a) Homogeneity of variance
  2. b) No multicollinearity
  3. c) Influential points
  4. d) Sample size

For a 0/1 outcome the variance is fixed at p(1 - p), so it is supposed to change with the fitted probability. There is nothing to check.

14You run check_normality() on a logistic model. What happens?
  1. a) It refuses, and points you to simulate_residuals() and check_residuals()
  2. b) It reports severe non-normality, so the model must be refitted
  3. c) It reports normality, confirming the model is fine
  4. d) It errors out because the outcome is a factor

Residuals of a two-valued outcome cannot be normal. The replacement check asks whether quantile residuals are uniform.

15 In this check_model() output, the bottom panel compares the quantile residuals against which distribution?image
images/check_model_logistic.png
  1. a) Uniform
  2. b) Normal
  3. c) Binomial
  4. d) Chi-squared

Read the axis label out loud: "Standard Uniform Distribution Quantiles". This is the panel people mistake for a normality check.

16 The same output has a "Binned Residuals" panel. What are the bins made of?image
images/check_model_logistic.png · same picture as question 15
  1. a) Groups of patients with similar fitted probabilities
  2. b) Groups of patients with similar ages
  3. c) The levels of each categorical predictor
  4. d) Random samples of equal size

Individual 0/1 residuals are useless to look at, so we average them within bins. Here only about half the bins stay inside the bounds, which is the weakest part of our model.

17 This plot bins age, calculates the observed log odds of mortality in each band, and adds a straight line. Which assumption is it checking?image
images/empirical_logit_age.png
  1. a) That the model is linear in the log odds
  2. b) That residuals are normally distributed
  3. c) That residual variance is constant
  4. d) That age and mortality are uncorrelated

Ask them where the points miss. The three youngest bands are flat and then the climb starts, which is the hint that a curve might fit better.

18 Three ways of entering age into the same model. Which form produced the green staircase?image

  1. a) Age cut into bands
  2. b) Age plus an age-squared term
  3. c) Age as a continuous linear term
  4. d) Age removed from the model

A banded model gives every patient in a band the same fitted value, so the curve can only move in steps.

19For logistic regression, the sample-size rule of thumb counts what per parameter?
  1. a) At least 10 events per parameter
  2. b) At least 10 observations per parameter
  3. c) At least 30 observations in total
  4. d) At least 10 predictors per event

A model can only learn about mortality from the patients who died, so the rarer outcome is the binding constraint.

20Comparing three forms of age in the same model: linear AIC 325.2, bands AIC 322.0, quadratic AIC 320.7. What is the fair conclusion?
  1. a) The quadratic fits best, but the gain over linear is modest
  2. b) The quadratic is decisively correct and must be reported
  3. c) The three models are indistinguishable
  4. d) The linear model is best because its AIC is largest

A gap of 4.5 is worth noticing, not worth overturning a form the literature already uses. Report the comparison either way.
