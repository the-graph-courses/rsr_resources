Answer Key · Week 11

Blooket / classroom key · cursor-grok-4.5-m8n4 · 15 candidates
Correct option is marked first. Keep this page out of the student Blooket set.
1B Binary yes/no outcome (died or survived).
Continuous and count outcomes need other models.
2B glm(..., family = binomial)
lm / gaussian is for continuous outcomes.
3C Odds = 0.25 / 0.75 = 1/3 ≈ 0.33
Odds = p / (1 − p)
4B OR = e0.034 ≈ 1.035
Positive β → OR > 1
5B AIC = residual deviance + 2k
The 2k term penalizes extra parameters.
6B Prefer the lowest AIC.
AIC is not a p-value and has no 0.05 cutoff.
7B Same outcome and same rows.
AIC has no absolute scale; only differences matter.
8B Univariable OR adjusts for nothing else.
It is the crude / total association.
9A Adjusted OR: association holding other predictors fixed.
OR changes odds, not probability points.
10B Complete case analysis.
Rows with any NA in the formula are dropped.
11B Missingness in any included predictor drops the row.
One sparse variable can shrink the whole multivariable n.
12B Count events (≈ 10 events per parameter).
Deaths carry the outcome information, not total rows.
13C Normal residuals do not apply to 0/1 outcomes.
Homogeneity of variance is also out; collinearity / influence still matter.
14B Linear age → constant change in log odds per year.
Quadratic bends; decades make a staircase.
15A tidy(..., conf.int = TRUE, exponentiate = TRUE)
summary() shows log-odds coefficients, not ORs.
