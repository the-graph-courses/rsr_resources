Simple linear regression with a binary predictor

When a predictor has exactly two groups, lm() draws a line between two group means. You will fit models, read coefficients as differences, and see how changing the reference level relabels (but does not change) the underlying relationship.

Overview

A binary predictor takes only two values, such as female vs male or smoker vs nonsmoker. In R you still write a familiar formula:

lm(outcome ~ group, data = my_data)

R turns the group into a dummy variable: 0 for the reference level and 1 for the other level. The intercept is the mean of the reference group; the slope is the difference in means between the two groups.

This practical uses teaching datasets from common R packages:

  • openintro::births14: US live births (birth weight by sex)
  • MASS::birthwt: Baystate birth weights (birth weight by smoking status)
  • DAAG::ais: Australian Institute of Sport athletes (serum ferritin by sex)

The equation

\[\hat{y} = b_0 + b_1 \cdot x\]

Reading the pieces for a binary predictor:

  • \(x = 0\) for the reference group; \(x = 1\) for the other group.
  • \(b_0\) = mean of \(y\) in the reference group.
  • \(b_1\) = difference in means (other group minus reference group).

With a binary predictor, the fitted line is just the segment joining the two group means.
