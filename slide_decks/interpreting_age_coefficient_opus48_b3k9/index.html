Interpreting the age coefficient

1
Weak grip by age: summary output

Last time we modeled the probability of weak grip. Each dot is one man in the data, at 1 (weak grip) or 0 (not weak). The gold curve is the fitted model.

#>             Estimate Std. Error z value Pr(>|z|)
#> (Intercept) -8.82431    0.31925  -27.64   <2e-16 ***
#> age          0.11683    0.00476   24.57   <2e-16 ***

The change in probability per year is not the same everywhere. It is small at young ages, larger in the middle, and small again at old ages.

$p = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-(\beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{age})}}$
$\widehat p = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-(-8.824 + 0.1168\,\text{age})}}$

The summary gives one number for age, $\beta_1 = 0.1168$. It is the change in the log-odds for each extra year, not a change in probability. The next panel shows what that means.

2
The same model on two scales

Below we show the derivation of the log-odds form from the probability form of the logistic equation.

$p = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-\eta}}, \qquad \eta = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{age}$

Subtract $p$ from 1:

$1-p = \dfrac{1+e^{-\eta}}{1+e^{-\eta}} - \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-\eta}} = \dfrac{e^{-\eta}}{1+e^{-\eta}}$

We now have two identities for $p$ and $1-p$. Substitute them into $p/(1-p)$ (to divide by a fraction, flip it and multiply):

$\dfrac{p}{1-p} = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-\eta}} \div \dfrac{e^{-\eta}}{1+e^{-\eta}}$
$= \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-\eta}}\cdot\dfrac{1+e^{-\eta}}{e^{-\eta}} = \dfrac{1}{e^{-\eta}} = e^{\eta}$

Take the natural log of both sides:

$\ln\!\dfrac{p}{1-p} = \ln\!\left(e^{\eta}\right) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{age}$

The log-odds form of the same model:

$\ln\!\dfrac{p}{1-p} = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{age}$
$\ln\!\dfrac{p}{1-p} = -8.824 + 0.1168\,\text{age}$

The curve becomes a straight line as the y-axis changes from probability to log-odds.

Each dashed line is one probability changed to a log-odds: find the odds $p/(1-p)$, then take $\ln$. Above $.5$ is positive, below $.5$ is negative.

A probability stays in 0 to 1. A log-odds runs from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, and corresponds to a straight line fit of our model.

Why use this form? The age effect is the slope of the line. If age increases by one year, how much does the log-odds increase? By 0.1168, the value of $\beta_1$ in the summary output.

3
$\beta_1$ is a (log) odds ratio

Each extra year adds a fixed $\beta_1 = 0.1168$ to the log-odds. To make that reportable, we exponentiate it. Adding on the log scale becomes multiplying on the odds scale, so $\beta_1$ turns into a multiplier on the odds:

$e^{\beta_1} = e^{0.1168} = 1.124 = \dfrac{\text{odds}_{a+1}}{\text{odds}_a}$

That multiplier is the odds ratio: each extra year multiplies the odds of weak grip by 1.124 (a 12% increase), the same at every age (see bars).

# get the odds ratio straight from the model
broom::tidy(weak_glm, exponentiate = TRUE, conf.int = TRUE)
#> term        estimate conf.low conf.high
#> age         1.124    1.114    1.135

The estimate is the odds ratio; conf.low / conf.high give its 95% CI. This is the number we report. (How to report it well is the next deck.)

4
Probability form and log-odds form

Logistic regression can be written in two forms. Each form is useful for a different purpose.

1. To predict a probability

Use the probability form (the logistic function). Put in an age, read out a probability.

$p = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(\beta_0+\beta_1\,\text{age})}}$

On this scale there is no single effect. The change in probability per year depends on the age.

2. To understand the effect

Use the log-odds form (a straight line).

$\ln\dfrac{p}{1-p} = \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\text{age}$

On this scale the effect is one number: $\beta_1 = 0.1168$ added to the log-odds each year.

A change in log-odds is hard to interpret directly. Exponentiate it instead. A difference of logs becomes a ratio, $e^{\ln A - \ln B} = A/B$, so the added $\beta_1$ is a multiplier on the odds: $e^{0.1168} = 1.124$, the odds ratio.
