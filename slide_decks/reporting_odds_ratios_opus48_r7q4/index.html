Reporting logistic regression: odds ratios and probabilities

1
Get the odds ratio out of R

The summary is on the log-odds scale. We exponentiate the coefficients to get odds ratios. broom::tidy does this in one step.

library(broom)
tidy(weak_glm, exponentiate = TRUE, conf.int = TRUE)
#> term        estimate std.error statistic  p.value conf.low conf.high
#> (Intercept) 0.000147   0.319      -27.6  <2e-16  7.83e-5  2.73e-4
#> age         1.124      0.00476    24.6   <2e-16  1.114    1.135

The estimate column is the odds ratio: 1.12 for age, with a 95% CI of 1.11 to 1.13. (By hand: exp(cbind(coef(weak_glm), confint(weak_glm))).)

2
Reading an odds ratio

An OR above 1 means higher odds; below 1 means lower odds; exactly 1 means no effect.

Say it as a percent. Higher: (OR − 1) × 100. Lower: (1 − OR) × 100. So 1.124 is a 12% increase in the odds per year.

Two anchors: OR = 2 is twice the odds (100% higher); OR = 0.5 is half the odds (50% lower).

An odds ratio is not a risk ratio. Do not say "12% more likely" or "twice as likely". Say "12% higher odds".

odds ratio → plain words
0.5half the odds · 50% lower
0.820% lower odds
1.0no change
1.1212% higher odds  (age, per year)
2.0twice the odds · 100% higher
3
Predicted probabilities

The odds ratio is the effect. For a concrete number a reader can picture, report a predicted probability: put an age into the model.

newdata <- data.frame(age = c(50, 60, 70, 80))
newdata$prob <- predict(weak_glm, newdata, type = "response")
newdata
#>   age    prob
#> 1  50  0.0482
#> 2  60  0.1401
#> 3  70  0.3439
#> 4  80  0.6277

type = "response" returns a probability, not a log-odds. So the model predicts about 14% at age 60 and 63% at age 80 (gold dots). For CIs on predictions, use marginaleffects::predictions().

4
Write it in a paper

One way to write the result

Among 4,184 Korean men aged 45 and older, older age was associated with higher odds of weak grip. Each additional year raised the odds by 12% (OR 1.12, 95% CI 1.11–1.13, p < 0.001). The predicted probability of weak grip rose from about 14% at age 60 to 63% at age 80.

Reporting checklist

  • Give the odds ratio and its 95% CI (and a p-value).
  • Say it in words: "12% higher odds", not "12% more likely".
  • Do not report the raw log-odds coefficient as the result.
  • Add a few predicted probabilities so readers can picture the size of the effect.
